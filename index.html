Why 3 satellites in this flat toy world? Unknowns: your x, your y, and your clock error. Three unknowns need three measurements. (Real GPS in 3D has x, y, z and clock, so it needs 4.)

Under the hood: the solver

Measured “pseudorange” = true distance + c·clock error. The solver guesses (x, y, b), computes residuals r = measured − (distance + b), builds the Jacobian (rows: unit vector away from the satellite, and 1 for the clock), and solves JᵀJ·Δ = Jᵀr. This is Gauss-Newton; the residual shrinks each iteration.

DOP is the multiplier from range error to position error: error ≈ DOP × timing noise. Computed as sqrt of diagonal entries of (JᵀJ)⁻¹ from the satellite directions. Ionosphere (about 7 m overhead, more near the horizon) and reflections push the whole cloud off-centre; noise sets its size; geometry stretches it.
